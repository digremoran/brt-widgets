Physical Limits of Silicon

Why Physics Will Eventually Overtake Today's AI Silicon Monopoly

Treating today's semiconductor giants as permanent centers of gravity misses the ultimate arbiter: physics. Shoving billions of electrons through copper and silicon at gigawatt scale inflicts a severe tax in electrical resistance, thermal throttling, and massive cooling overhead.

Much like Thomas Edison's direct current (DC) lost its dominance to Tesla's alternating current (AC) when distance and line losses exposed DC's physical ceiling, current electrical interconnects face an inescapable wall. Moving signals through glass and photons changes the fundamental thermal and transport energy math by orders of magnitude.

The De Gaulle & Physics Rule "The graveyard is filled with indispensable men—and indispensable technologies." No corporate incumbent holds a permanent lease on physics. When copper interconnect heat taxes eat your returns, light in glass takes over.
BRT Editorial Blurb Ready to Share
"Everyone treats today’s AI silicon bottleneck as permanent because the capital and hype are massive. Yet pushing electrons through copper and silicon at gigawatt scale runs straight into resistance, heat, and cooling limits that scale painfully. Moving signal transport and compute toward glass and photons—much like AC overtaking Edison’s DC—changes the energy and thermal math by orders of magnitude where electrical interconnects choke. No incumbent is indispensable when the physics shifts."
